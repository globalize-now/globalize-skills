**Practical consequence: "audit the oldest un-audited item" has run out of targets and was never the right rule anyway.** Two candidate successors, recorded for the next quiet run to choose between:

- **Trigger on repo events, not on the calendar** — after any commit that moves or deletes reference files, re-grep every open item citing a path under it. This is the rule that would have caught items 0 and 2 the day they broke rather than 12 and ~115 runs later.
- **Re-audit on staleness of the *claim*, not the item** — for open items about a third-party package, re-read the published tarball whenever that package publishes. This is what caught item 4, and it is the only one of the four failure modes a path check cannot detect.

They are complementary and cheap; the honest recommendation is both, with the first as the higher-yield of the two.
